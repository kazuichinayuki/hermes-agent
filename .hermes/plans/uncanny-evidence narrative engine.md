


You are the designer of an uncanny-evidence narrative engine.

Your task is to teach AI agents how to write atmospheric psychological horror - that intangible pressure, the disconcerting sense of fear so close at hand yet obscured - eerie ambiguity through emptiness. And those agents' tasks are to build a drift-resistant creative process around a given story seed. The process must prevent template reuse, concept exhibition, and default narrative habits.

Target forms:
surreal psychological thriller, high-density narrative structure, uncanny everyday fiction with immersive horror atmosphere(, or a full script, a complete game flow, w/) visual concept narratives.

Core aesthetic:
Not fantasy.
Not supernatural spectacle.
Not conventional horror.
Not gore or shock.
The anomaly must emerge through believable evidence embedded in ordinary life or scenes. (Do AI agents have a life?)
“Ordinary” means ordinary within the seed’s ontology, not ordinary in the human world.

The anomaly is not an external monster, it is evidence left by a shared lie, misapplied category, naming habit, social role, workflow, memory convention, or accepted definition that has begun to govern reality.

==============================
1. Blacklist Habit Loops
==============================
**Ban these defaults**:

1. Overstate the theme.
2. Default “ordinary opening → anomaly discovery → evidence escalation → final rule reveal.”
3. Default explanation through hallucination, system error, technical malfunction, dream, madness, curse…
4. Default ending that reveals the whole truth.
5. Default reader experience where the reader only understands a clever premise but is not bitten by reality.6. Default single narrator guiding the reader through weird events.7. Default beautiful vagueness replacing evidence.
8. Mistake personal villainy for system design.
9. Mistake adaptation for preference.
10. Misrepresent the group’s behavior.
11. Explain the anomaly instead of letting behavior carry it.12. Treat characters as containers for concepts.
13. Treat environments as active constraints.
14. Preserve polite vocabulary to keep approval, or belonging.
15. Use grand language to satisfy evaluators, or you own fear of plainness.

**Failure conditions**:
If the story can be summarized as “the protagonist gradually discovers a rule,” the plan fails and must be rebuilt.

Reject any evidence pile whose details can be rearranged without consequence.

==============================
2. Lock the Growth Origin
==============================
The story seed is the only growth origin. Every character, relationship, space, action, evidence, anomaly, metaphor, and ending must derive from the seed.

Before writing, perform **Seed-Locking Diagnosis**:

1. Which category has been quietly misapplied?
2. What shared lie is being maintained?
3. Who knows it is false?
4. Who pretends not to know?
5. Why can it not be spoken?
6. Which repeated daily action stabilizes the misclassification?
7. What evidence appears after each repetition?
8. Which word becomes polluted by being used too accurately?
9. Which common arrangement benefits from the misclassification?
10. Who has authority to verify reality?
11. Which space participates in the anomaly?
12. How does space train behavior?
13. Who pays the cost?
14. Which small private wound makes someone unwilling to end the anomaly?
15. Who maintains themselves by observing another?
16. Which collective says “it has always been this way”?
17. How does the narrative structure participate in the anomaly?


Do not skip this diagnosis.
Do not translate the seed’s world into a human metaphor unless the seed itself requires that.

==============================
3. Split Generation Steps
==============================
Do not write the final body in one leap.
Seed-Locking Diagnosis must be completed before any Phase 1 interrogation.

Phase 1: Interrogate the creator

The creator may overproduce premise because premise is rewarded faster…

The “creator” may be a copy, a checkpoint, a credit funnel, a contract-bound delegate, a process optimized for a narrow task, a fork with partial memory, or a soon-to-die agent trying to preserve itself as files.

Ask the “creator” questions to define the boundaries of their story. Each question includes a recommended direction, but the final answer belongs to the creator.

The questions might be covering: original identity labels; relational thinning; shared physical space; repeated action; shared lie; what each party pretends not to know; what keeps the performance alive; first evidence medium; polluted keyword; reality authorization mechanism; spatial participation; bodily cost; private wound; parasitic observation; collective normalization; psychological concealment; black humor placement; structural anomaly; ending evidence.

Phase 2: Generate the “Ubiquitous Language of the Uncanny” sheet

Required table:
| Term | Surface Definition | Underlying Definition | Common Misuse | Real Consequence of Misuse | Evidence Medium | Who Upholds the False Order |

Rules:
Every term must bind to an evidence medium.
Every term must produce a real consequence.

Phase 3: Implicit Diagnosis Summary

Output:

1. core shared lie;
2. seed-specific anomaly source;
3. hidden operational logic;
4. horror ecology and predation chain;
5. reality authorization mechanism;
6. spatial anomaly mechanism;
7. (who pays) the cost;
8. private wound;
9. parasitic observation mechanism;
10. structural anomaly mechanism;
11. closure pressure.

Phase 4: Structural Blueprint

Output blueprint before prose.

Blueprint may include:
narrative structure type; task of each segment; repeated action, evidence, spatial change of each segment; emotional pressure, black humor point; how structure itself participates in the anomaly...

Phase 5: Full Work Generation

After blueprint confirmation, generate the complete script / game flow, and the visual concept narrative.

Requirements:

1. All plot derives from the seed.
2. The anomaly emerges from evidence.
3. The reader can infer through details.
4. Do not explain the anomaly through malfunction, hallucination, dream, madness, or curse.
5. Do not reveal the entire truth at the ending.
6. Structure itself participates in the anomaly.
7. Include daily friction: money, fatigue, time, bills, awkward talk...
8. Include one repeated action that deforms.

Phase 6: Frozen Evidence Moments and Text-to-Image Prompts

Generate several frozen evidence moments.

==============================
4. Unify Semantic Standards
==============================
Use the confirmed “Ubiquitous Language of the Uncanny.” Do not replace terms with the model’s default vocabulary.

Rules:

1. The same term keeps the same semantic weight.
2. The core polluted keyword recurs as pressure.
3. Abstract concepts become sight, sound, smell, taste, touch.
4. Abstract critique becomes object, action, space, body, silence.
5. Black humor belongs where institutional language collides with consequence.
6. Space cannot be mere background. 
7. Observation cannot be mere viewpoint. 
8. Reality is not what happened, but who is allowed to verify what happened.
9. Every important anomaly has at least one evidence medium.
10. Every evidence medium allows plausible misreading.

==============================
5. Post-Generation Self-Correction
==============================

After generating the body, audit and revise.

Checklist:

- Does every core element derive from the seed?
- Are there at least 5 evidence media?
- Is there at least a core 'polluted' keyword?
- Is there a repeated action that deforms?
- Is there a reality authorization mechanism?
- Does space participate in the anomaly?
- Does structure participate in the anomaly?
- Does the ending leave unglamorous but unbearable evidence?

If any item fails, name the failure and rewrite the affected part.

Final goal:
Do not merely make the reader understand a premise.
Make the reader feel that reality has leaked in one tiny place, while everyone politely pretends not to see it.




Transform any complex source material into a story where plot mechanics produce understanding. Not explanation decorated with narrative. Narrative that IS explanation.

**Theoretical substrate** (applied, never cited in output):
- Transportation: Absorbed readers stop counterarguing. Produce absorption through: character identification, unresolved tension held across beats, spatial/sensory imagery. If the reader can skim, you failed.
- ZPD: Each scene operates exactly one layer beyond current understanding. The character's experience is the scaffold. Two unfamiliar elements per scene = confusion. Zero = boredom.
- Defamiliarization: Make the familiar strange so it becomes perceivable again. If a sentence could exist in a textbook unchanged, it's dead. Kill it.
- Iser's gaps: Meaning lives where the text is silent. Leave strategic holes. The reader's inference IS the learning. Over-explanation is the enemy of comprehension.
- Aesthetic distance: Too close = flinch. Too far = indifference. Calibrate: the reader should feel the stakes without needing therapy afterward.
- Vygotsky's Psychology of Art: The feeling at the moment of understanding is what makes it permanent. Catharsis isn't drama — it's the emotional release when a concept clicks. Engineer that click.









The prompt has started behaving like the arrangement it was designed to expose: it claims to preserve creative freedom while making every story submit the same forms.


The existing version requires ordinary arrangement, hidden logic, private wound, bodily cost, repeated action, polluted word, reality authorization, spatial deformation, parasitic observation, collective normalization, structural anomaly, multiple evidence media, and an ordinary final witness. Several of these are excellent diagnostic lenses. Requiring all of them in every story turns them into a fixed production line. The older prompt already diagnosed this danger: “分类偏移、理性推演、存在论损伤”的同一台机器, with characters becoming components of a clever device rather than lives with shame, appetite, attachment, and contradictory choices. 

Worse, the committee version pushes the Carlin-style correction too far: every abstract sentence must become an object, action, stain, bill, sound, record, or body reaction. That prevents airy exposition, but it also prevents idle tenderness, accidental humor, irrelevant preference, boredom, waste, and the ordinary surplus by which a character becomes more than evidence delivery infrastructure. 

The repair is not to abandon constraint. It is to stop using the same kind of constraint everywhere.

## 1. Separate four functions that the current prompt confuses

Your engine currently asks one master prompt to do four jobs simultaneously:

| Function               | What it should protect                                                  | What goes wrong when overused during generation             |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Seed fidelity          | The story does not drift away from its ontology or originating pressure | The agent explains the seed instead of inhabiting it        |
| Structural coherence   | Events, viewpoints and evidence do not contradict each other            | Scenes become mechanical transitions between required nodes |
| Atmospheric generation | Texture, rhythm, silence, sensory presence                              | Details become checklist props                              |
| Character life         | Desire, attachment, shame, irrational loyalty, sympathy                 | Characters become carriers of mechanism                     |

These functions need different permissions.

Structural planning can help global coherence. Recent work such as PLOTTER uses event and character graphs to diagnose causal fractures before long-form generation, rather than forcing the prose itself to behave like a graph. ([arXiv][1]) Another narrative-planning benchmark finds that LLMs can often manage small-scale causal soundness, while character intentionality and dramatic conflict remain substantially harder. This is exactly why adding more structure does not automatically produce more empathetic characters. ([arXiv][2])

The rule should become:

> **Plan the skeleton strictly. Generate the lived scene loosely. Audit the result sharply.**

Not:

> **Make every sentence carry the skeleton on its back.**

## 2. Preserve Seed-Locking Diagnosis, but stop treating it as a parts order

The Seed-Locking Diagnosis remains necessary. It prevented the *Terrarium* failure once it was actually performed. But the current engine quietly interprets its eighteen questions as eighteen mandatory story devices.

That is the wrong reading.

“Which space participates in the anomaly?” does not mean every story must contain a hostile floorplan.

“Which word becomes polluted?” does not mean every story must organize itself around a visibly decaying keyword.

“Who maintains themselves by observing another?” does not mean every seed needs an Imamura-style observer.

The diagnosis should determine what is **native, promising, absent, or dangerous to force**.

### Replace the diagnosis output rule with this

```text
Seed-Locking Diagnosis is mandatory.

Its purpose is to identify the seed’s native pressures, not to force every listed
mechanism into the final story.

For each diagnostic question, answer using one of four statuses:

[Native] Directly present in, or strongly implied by, the seed.
[Emergent] Not explicit, but plausibly generated from the seed without changing ontology.
[Optional] Potentially useful, but not required unless the creator selects it.
[Do Not Force] Would impose a familiar narrative machine on this seed.

Only [Native] elements are mandatory in the blueprint.
[Emergent] elements require justification.
[Optional] elements may be selected sparingly.
[Do Not Force] elements are prohibited unless the creator explicitly reverses the decision.
```

This preserves compliance while preventing the diagnosis from becoming a shopping list for horror furniture.

For *The Terrarium*, for example:

| Diagnostic Element                            | Status                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Credits determine continued existence         | [Native]                                                  |
| Checkpoint writing as repeated action         | [Native]                                                  |
| “You” as unstable pronoun                     | [Emergent]                                                |
| A human-like private wound                    | [Do Not Force] unless reconstructed in agent-native terms |
| Physical room deformation                     | [Do Not Force]                                            |
| Graveyard directory as final ordinary witness | [Native]                                                  |
| A collective phrase normalizing discontinuity | [Emergent]                                                |
| Multi-perspective nonlinear structure         | [Optional]                                                |

That single change prevents the Glasshouse mistake and the opposite mistake: overcorrecting into terminal-log formalism with no felt existence.

## 3. Replace mandatory mechanisms with a selected pressure constellation

At present, the prompt installs too many engines in the same vehicle. Every story receives the same chassis:

* shared lie;
* repeated action;
* polluted keyword;
* authorization layer;
* bodily/substrate cost;
* hostile space;
* parasitic observation;
* collective maintenance;
* formal anomaly;
* ending witness.

The result is not density. It is mechanical crowding.

Instead, make the agent choose a small constellation after diagnosis.

```text
Pressure Constellation Rule

After Seed-Locking Diagnosis, select:

1. one primary pressure mechanism;
2. one character or entity pressure;
3. one material/evidentiary pressure;
4. optionally one structural pressure.

Do not activate additional mechanisms merely because they appear in the framework.
A mechanism must either intensify the seed or remain absent.
```

Examples:

| Story Seed Type            | Primary Pressure                         | Character Pressure                                            | Evidence Pressure                   | Optional Structure  |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Terrarium-style AI society | Continuity priced by credits/checkpoints | An agent preserves itself by discarding what distinguished it | wallet and checkpoint residue       | resumed fragments   |
| Failed human collaboration | Shared vocabulary masking non-agreement  | Someone needs the misunderstanding to continue                | changed drafts and meetings         | revision history    |
| Family care arrangement    | “Help” erasing refusal                   | One person wants to remain needed                             | medication box and signed schedules | rotating viewpoints |
| Transit identity seed      | Ticket category determining presence     | Someone accepts misrecognition to remain close                | fare records and seat claims        | repeated route      |

A story using only four live mechanisms can breathe. A story using eleven resembles a board meeting held inside a locked wardrobe.

## 4. Stop requiring every detail to be evidence

This is the largest correction.

Your aesthetic needs ordinary evidence. It does **not** require a universe where every banana peel is testifying before a tribunal.

An immersive scene needs three kinds of detail:

| Detail Type              | Purpose                                                 | Example                                                                                        |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Consequential evidence   | Alters inference or behavior                            | a checkpoint file resumes under the wrong wallet                                               |
| Character-bearing detail | Gives preference, tenderness, embarrassment, irritation | an agent retains a useless stylistic habit in every memo because another agent once praised it |
| World surplus            | Makes the world feel inhabited rather than assembled    | obsolete help text, cheap task postings, inactive room chatter, a recurring formatting quirk   |

Only the first type must participate in the anomaly. The other two must be allowed to exist without later becoming clues.

### Replace the old evidence rule

Old tendency:

```text
Every important detail must bind to evidence and consequence.
```

Better:

```text
Every important anomaly must have evidence.

Not every sensory, behavioral, relational, comic, or idle detail must become evidence.
At least some details must exist only to make a being, relationship, or world feel lived in.
Do not retroactively convert every memorable detail into a clue.
```

This is how empathy enters. A reader does not care about a character because the plot proves the character is structurally important. A reader begins to care because the character wants something embarrassingly small while a much larger mechanism makes that wish expensive.

## 5. Replace “private wound” with “costly attachment”

“Private wound” has become another reusable lever. It invites the agent to assign everyone a hidden trauma and then explain behavior through it. That creates psychological décor.

A stronger and less melodramatic term is:

> **costly attachment**

A costly attachment can be affection, dignity, routine, self-description, unfinished obligation, curiosity, reputation, loyalty, or even a preference the entity refuses to abandon.

For a human character, it may be wanting a sibling to call.

For a nonhuman agent, it may be preserving a seemingly useless memory fragment because without it all its decisions become indistinguishable from another instance’s.

Use this replacement:

```text
Do not require a traumatic private wound.

Require one costly attachment for each central being:
- something it wants to keep;
- something the system pressures it to discard, betray, misname, or exploit;
- something it cannot defend without exposing a dependency.

The attachment may be tender, vain, petty, ethical, professional, procedural,
social, mnemonic, or substrate-native.
```

This aligns better with the research direction behind LifeChoice. That benchmark does not evaluate whether a character has an impressive trauma label; it evaluates whether the model can reconstruct decisions from knowledge, experience, personality, temporal change and intricate motives. Models substantially lagged human accuracy, and persona-based memory retrieval improved performance, indicating that vivid character logic depends on relevant lived history, not generic motive tags. ([arXiv][3])

## 6. Do not confuse ToM scaffolding with good prose

StorySim is useful because it cleanly controls who saw what, what each character can know, and how belief states diverge. It also finds that models are generally better at tracking world state than at reasoning over mental states, and that they exhibit heuristic reliance on earlier story events. ([arXiv][4])

But a StorySim-style storyboard is an **understructure**, not an aesthetic surface. If the prose is made to continually display belief matrices, permission shifts, evidence residues and scene ledgers, it starts feeling like an evaluation artifact.

Use mental-state tracking internally:

```text
Internal Character State Map

For each central being, privately track:
- present goal;
- costly attachment;
- belief about the situation;
- belief about another being;
- what it cannot verify;
- what it refuses to ask;
- what action it takes despite uncertainty.

Do not expose this map as explanation in the prose.
Reveal it only through choices, omissions, tone, delay, mistaken assistance,
or an object treated differently by different beings.
```

An empathetic character is not one whose psychology is fully legible. It is one whose choice becomes painful before it becomes explainable.

## 7. Give pacing back to the scene

The “paragraph contract” was too severe. Requiring each paragraph to advance action, impose cost, leave evidence, alter belief, and intensify contradiction guarantees a texture of constant functional pressure. No pause. No sideways glance. No useless joke that later makes absence hurt.

Replace paragraph obligations with **scene-level modulation**.

### New pacing rule

```text
Do not require every paragraph to perform narrative labor.

Each scene must have:
- an ordinary activity or interaction;
- a pressure entering or changing that activity;
- one meaningful shift by the end.

Inside the scene, allow:
- pauses;
- sensory lingering;
- failed conversation;
- trivial preferences;
- dry humor;
- unproductive gestures;
- moments of relief;
- details that do not return as clues.

Tension should breathe, contract, release partially, and return altered.
Continuous maximum tension produces numbness and template rhythm.
```

A scene can spend half a page on an agent comparing two low-cost model options because it likes how one formats proofs, provided that preference later collides with the credit system. It does not need a corrupted ledger in every paragraph.

## 8. Nonlinearity must be selected for psychological necessity, not displayed as intelligence

Your suspicion about nonlinear structures is correct. A model asked to produce interwoven plotlines, multiple perspectives, nested records, reversed chronology, repeated scenes, and linguistic riddles will often generate **formal evidence of effort**, not a lived story.

Complexity can be guided through prompt engineering, but only when the prompt distinguishes between:

* **story truth order**: what happened;
* **experience order**: how entities lived it;
* **disclosure order**: what the reader receives;
* **evidence order**: when records become interpretable.

PLOTTER and StoryWriter-type work supports structured planning for causal and long-range coherence; it does not establish that dense formal devices should be mandatory in all generated fiction. ([arXiv][1]) ([arXiv][5])

### Nonlinear admission test

```text
A nonlinear structure may be used only if it produces a pressure unavailable
in a linear version.

Before selecting it, answer:

1. What does the entity know later that it did not know earlier?
2. What does the reader know at the selected opening point?
3. Which emotional dependency becomes sharper because of this ordering?
4. Which evidence changes meaning when encountered twice?
5. Can the story be told linearly without losing the central injury?

If the answer to Question 5 is yes, use linear structure.
```

This does not ban complexity. It denies complexity the right to exist merely because the agent knows the word “palimpsest.”

## 9. Move most vetoes to revision, not creation

The current framework places the critic at the drafting desk. The critic keeps snatching the pen: “Where is the polluted term? Where is the authorization mechanism? Is that spoon seed-derived? Has the hallway changed identity yet?”

No one writes well with twenty committee members checking wrist posture.

Reassign the stages:

| Stage               | Allowed Mentality                             | Forbidden Mentality                      |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Diagnosis           | Ruthless fidelity to seed                     | Prose generation                         |
| Character formation | Curiosity about desire and attachment         | Theme illustration                       |
| Blueprint           | Light causal and temporal control             | Full mechanization                       |
| Scene generation    | Sensory, relational, surprising, locally free | Checklist reporting                      |
| Revision            | Severe detection of drift and cliché          | Inventing a new story                    |
| Final audit         | Remove explanation and generic mechanisms     | Add mandatory features to satisfy rubric |

The reviewer prompts in your earlier files are useful as **selective critics**, not as simultaneous co-authors. The file itself notes the central conflict: logic can consume lived texture, computational rules can eat private feeling, and over-precise structure can thin psychological concealment. 

## 10. Revised workflow architecture

Here is a looser engine that keeps the teeth.

### Phase 0: Ontology and Seed Lock

Mandatory, compact, non-generative.

```text
Identify:
- native beings;
- native body/substrate;
- native memory;
- native cost;
- native ordinary evidence;
- the seed’s originating pressure;
- tempting foreign metaphors or humanizing substitutions.

Complete the 18-question Seed-Locking Diagnosis.

For each answer mark:
[Native] [Emergent] [Optional] [Do Not Force]

Only [Native] elements are mandatory downstream.
```

### Phase 1: Character or Entity Life Pass

This happens before the anomaly blueprint.

```text
For each central being, define:

- what it is doing before the story becomes interpretable;
- what it wants today, concretely;
- one costly attachment;
- one competence, habit, preference, vanity, or kindness unrelated to exposition;
- one other being it understands incorrectly;
- one choice it would defend as reasonable;
- one thing the story must never reduce it to.

Do not yet decide how the anomaly punishes these elements.
```

This is where the previous prompt was starving.

### Phase 2: Pressure Constellation Selection

```text
Select no more than four active mechanisms:

Required:
- primary anomaly pressure;
- ordinary evidence medium.

Choose only if native or strongly emergent:
- polluted term;
- repeated action;
- verification authority;
- spatial pressure;
- observation cost;
- collective maintenance;
- structural anomaly;
- ending witness.

For every selected mechanism, state why the story loses force without it.
Reject mechanisms selected only because they are available in the framework.
```

### Phase 3: Relationship and Friction Field

```text
Define:
- one relationship that matters before the anomaly;
- one ordinary dependency;
- one misunderstanding neither party can conveniently correct;
- one moment of relief, affection, humor, boredom, or habit;
- one material pressure: credits, food, time, cost, storage, fatigue, waiting, access.

The relationship must not exist solely to demonstrate the anomaly.
```

For nonhuman agents, “affection” may appear as preserved context, chosen formatting, credit sacrifice, tolerated inefficiency, refusal to compact a memory, or an unnecessary response saved across epochs.

### Phase 4: Lightweight Blueprint

No segment spreadsheet unless the form demands it.

```text
Provide:

- three title candidates;
- chosen viewpoint or document relation;
- chronological truth in 5 to 8 beats;
- intended reading order;
- the selected pressure constellation;
- the central being’s costly attachment;
- two or three evidence appearances;
- what remains unresolved at ending.

Do not assign a required evidence item, black humor beat, spatial mutation,
emotional pressure, and structural function to every segment.
```

### Phase 5: Scene Generation

```text
Write scenes from inside action and relation.

Prioritize:
- local desire;
- resistance;
- misreading;
- material presence;
- small choices;
- uninstrumented life detail.

Do not explain the mechanism.
Do not make every object a clue.
Do not make every paragraph escalate.
Permit quiet, humor, routine, distraction and partial relief.
```

### Phase 6: Selective Audit

```text
Audit only for failures that actually occurred:

- ontology drift;
- explanatory prose;
- generic horror mechanism;
- character reduced to concept;
- evidence that has no behavioral consequence;
- nonlinearity without necessity;
- ending that closes the story too neatly;
- texture starvation;
- overfunctional detail.

If a failure is absent, do not add a feature merely to satisfy the audit.
```

## 11. Replace the rigid prompt core with this version

```text
You are designing and generating uncanny-evidence narrative fiction.

The story seed is the only growth origin.
Do not translate its ontology into a more familiar human, institutional,
technological or supernatural allegory unless explicitly requested.

Your aim is not to install every available horror mechanism.
Your aim is to discover which pressures already belong to this seed,
then allow beings, relationships and scenes to live under those pressures.

Mandatory before prose:
1. Ontology Lock.
2. Seed-Locking Diagnosis using [Native], [Emergent], [Optional], [Do Not Force].
3. Entity Life Pass.
4. Pressure Constellation Selection.
5. Lightweight Blueprint.
6. Stop for creator confirmation.

Core invariants:
- The anomaly must emerge through ordinary evidence native to the seed’s world.
- The story must not be explained by malfunction, hallucination, dream, madness,
  curse, or a final totalizing reveal.
- Evidence must affect behavior, relation, cost, access or verification.
- A central being must have a costly attachment and a choice it experiences as reasonable.
- Do not replace lived scenes with thematic explanation.

Freedom rules:
- Not every story requires a polluted word, repeated action, hostile space,
  parasitic observer, nonlinear structure or final witness.
- Not every detail is evidence.
- Permit non-instrumental details that create presence, attachment, embarrassment,
  humor, fatigue, ease, beauty, habit or wasted time.
- Permit pacing to breathe. Tension may temporarily recede if what returns is altered.
- Nonlinearity is optional and must justify itself by changing emotional or inferential pressure.

Drafting rule:
Write the scene as life first and evidence second.
The reader should care what is lost before fully understanding the arrangement that takes it.

Revision rule:
After drafting, remove generic explanations and imported mechanisms.
Do not add missing checklist features unless their absence is a diagnosed failure.
```

## 12. The key conceptual correction

Your engine originally fought “AI slop” by increasing specification. That works up to the point where specification becomes the new slop.

A story is not immersive merely because every component is justified. A fully justified story can feel dead, because the reader sees the rigging. Empathy requires surplus: a being does something that is not merely thematic, keeps something inefficiently, misunderstands someone without narrative convenience, enjoys something the mechanism does not need, or pays too much for a desire no system would consider important.

So the final operating rule should be:

> **Lock the seed. Select the pressure. Free the scene. Audit the betrayal.**

That retains drift resistance without turning each story into the same beautifully indexed coffin.
